Key calculated values

Available Stock = max(0, On-hand Stock - Reserved Stock)
Line Subtotal = Quantity × Unit Price × (1 - Discount% / 100)
Change = Amount Paid - Final Sale Total

Cost price, SRP, line price, and subtotal must remain distinct. Cost supports profit decisions; SRP/price supports customer charges; subtotal records realized line revenue.

4. POS and inventory transaction

  1. Load only active products with sellable inventory.
  2. Select the exact variant: model, color, department, and size.
  3. Validate requested quantity against available stock.
  4. Resolve one applicable promotion and calculate discount.
  5. Select or create a customer; enforce valid and unique email when details are saved.
  6. Validate payment and manager override when required.
  7. Call complete_sale with sale line details.
  8. Commit sale, payment, stock deduction, and logs atomically.
  9. Store the actual applied promo_id on the sale line.
  10. Print receipt and refresh dependent queries.
Atomicity: a receipt must not exist without the corresponding stock deduction, and stock must not be deducted without a committed sale. The database transaction is the authoritative safeguard.

6. Promotion overlap and attribution

Promotions do not stack. For each cart line, the POS selects one winner:

  1. Product-specific target outranks category-specific target.
  2. Category-specific target outranks All Products.
  3. For equal specificity: BOGO outranks Bundle, Fixed, then Percentage.
  4. For equal specificity and type, the larger effective discount wins.

Overlapping dates are acceptable when product scopes do not intersect. A storewide promotion overlaps every product, so product-specific rules will override it under the current specificity rule.

Recommended governance: permit disjoint target overlaps, block duplicate active scope/date conflicts, allow only one active All Products campaign at a time, and display the winning rule before saving.

Performance attribution

  • New sale lines count toward the campaign identified by recorded promo_id.
  • Legacy rows without promo_id require a positive discount before inference.
  • Ordinary target-product sales during a campaign window are not automatically promotion sales.
  • Only completed sales contribute to attributed promotion revenue and units.

7. Sales Forecast computed from actual data

What “actual data” means: the forecast is recalculated from current completed POS transactions fetched from Supabase. It is not populated with hard-coded example values.

7.1 Actual-data extraction

  1. useSales() loads sales_transaction with payment and sale details.
  2. isCompletedSale retains completed/paid/successful payments.
  3. Cancelled, canceled, and fully returned sales are excluded.
  4. Revenue uses adjusted_total_amount, then total_amount, then original_total_amount.
  5. Units are summed from sales_details.quantity.
  6. The transaction timestamp is assigned to its Manila business date.
  7. Every missing calendar day is inserted with revenue = 0 and units = 0.
sales_transaction + payment + sales_details ↓ completed-status filter Actual sale amount and actual quantities ↓ Manila business-date grouping Map<YYYY-MM-DD, { revenue, units }> ↓ fill missing calendar dates with zero Continuous daily actual series ↓ average + trend + scenario calculations Future revenue and unit projections

7.2 History and horizons

ViewDisplayed historyForecast horizon

QuarterlyAbout 8 quarters4 quarters
Annually5 years3 years

The model baseline uses up to 90 calendar days, beginning later when the earliest sale is more recent. The historical chart now shows zero-sale periods so it does not connect separated transactions as continuous activity.

7.3 Baseline and trend

Average Daily Revenue = Σ Actual Daily Revenue / Calendar-Day Count
Average Daily Units = Σ Actual Daily Units / Calendar-Day Count
Raw Trend = (Second-Half Average - First-Half Average) / First-Half Average
Daily Trend = clamp(Raw Trend, -0.25, +0.25)

If the earlier-half average is zero, trend is set to zero.

7.4 Projection

Multiplier[h] = max(0.10, 1 + Daily Trend × (h / 2))
Projected Revenue[h] = round(Average Daily Revenue × Days in Period × Multiplier[h])
Projected Units[h] = round(Average Daily Units × Days in Period × Multiplier[h])

7.5 Higher and lower cases

Period Trend = clamp((Recent-Half Average - Earlier-Half Average) / Earlier-Half Average, -0.35, +0.35)
Scenario Spread = clamp(|Period Trend| + 0.15, 0.12, 0.45)
Higher = round(Base × (1 + Spread)); Lower = max(0, round(Base × (1 - Spread)))

With insufficient history, spread is 35%.

7.6 Probability and confidence

Signal = 0.60 × Period Trend + 0.40 × Period Momentum
Volatility = min(1, Standard Deviation / Average Period Revenue)
Probability Confidence = History Quality × max(0.35, 1 - 0.35 × Volatility)
Higher Probability[h] = clamp(50 + round(Signal × 140 × Probability Confidence × Horizon Decay[h]), 20, 80)
Displayed Confidence = min(90, max(55, 52 + 7 × Forecast-Base Period Count))

Insufficient-history confidence is 35%. These are heuristic planning scores, not statistically calibrated confidence intervals.

Example with a no-sale day

If Day 3 had no completed sale, its actual revenue and units must be zero. It remains in the denominator:

Average Daily Revenue = (1,000 + 1,200 + 0 + 1,400 + 1,600 + 1,800) / 6 = PHP 1,166.67

Removing Day 3 would incorrectly divide by five and produce PHP 1,400, overstating the store’s calendar-day baseline by 20%.

9. Forecast strengths, limitations, and improvements

Strengths

  • Uses current completed POS data.
  • Includes zero-sale calendar days.
  • Caps extreme changes.
  • Supports several horizons.
  • Transparent and inexpensive.

Limitations

  • No explicit holiday, payday, weather, or seasonality variables.
  • No explicit promotion or price feature.
  • Stockouts can hide demand.
  • Trend multiplier may become aggressive over long horizons.
  • Scenario/probability/confidence values are heuristic.
  • The current model does not publish formal backtest errors beside the chart.

Best upgrades

  1. Persist each forecast before the predicted period.
  2. Calculate MAE, RMSE, WAPE, and bias.
  3. Compare against naive and seasonal-naive baselines.
  4. Learn scenario ranges from residuals.
  5. Add stockout, weekday, promotion, and seasonal indicators.
  6. Separate baseline demand from promotional lift.

10. Security, audit, and reliability

  • Authentication: verifies the user.
  • Authorization: checks permitted role/action.
  • Application session: sent through x-meryl-session and resolved by database policies.
  • RLS: protects table rows even when UI checks are bypassed.
  • Manager override: required for discounts above configured limits.
  • Audit: actor, action, entity, old/new values, metadata, and timestamp.
  • Atomic RPC: protects sale, stock, payment, and replacement consistency.
  • Migrations: constraints and functions must be deployed in controlled order and verified.
Security rule: frontend validation is never the final authority. Sensitive rules must be repeated or enforced in PostgreSQL/Flask.

12. Defense questions and answers

What is the source of truth?
Committed PostgreSQL records. UI state and charts are derived and must refetch after mutations.

How does a sale affect inventory?
The atomic sale function creates the transaction and detail/payment records, validates stock and discount, deducts inventory, and writes movement evidence in one database transaction.

Why do you show no-sale days?
The forecast models calendar performance. Skipping empty days overstates the average and visually connects unrelated sales.

Is Sales Forecast machine learning?
No. It is a transparent time-series heuristic using actual daily averages, capped trend, momentum, volatility, and history coverage.

Does promotion discount stack?
No. One promotion wins per product line using specificity, type priority, and effective discount.

How is promotion performance made accurate?
New sale lines store the applied campaign ID. Only completed attributed lines are counted.

What prevents duplicate customers?
Normalized UI validation plus a case-insensitive unique email index in PostgreSQL.

What prevents overselling?
UI availability checks improve usability, while the atomic database function is the authoritative concurrent stock check.

Is the forecast confidence statistical?
No. It is a history-coverage heuristic. Formal confidence needs residual-based backtesting.

What would you improve first?
Persist and backtest forecasts, report WAPE/bias, and centralize forecast logic as one tested service.

Study checklist

  • Trace one sale from cart to receipt, inventory, reports, and analytics.
  • Draw the core table relationships without notes.
  • Explain available vs on-hand vs reserved stock.
  • Explain promotion winner selection and attribution.
  • Recalculate the PHP 13,652 forecast example.
  • Explain why missing calendar days must equal zero.
  • Name forecast limitations and measurable improvements.
  • Explain authentication, authorization, RLS, audit, and atomicity.
